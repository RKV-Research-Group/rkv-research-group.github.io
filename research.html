Theme 01

Fibre-reinforced concrete & constitutive modelling

Adding discrete steel or synthetic fibres to concrete changes how it cracks: instead of a sudden loss of strength, the fibres bridge cracks and give a gradual, ductile response. Capturing this behaviour reliably is essential if fibre-reinforced concrete (FRC) is to be used for structural members rather than only for slabs and overlays.

This theme develops constitutive laws for strain-softening and strain-hardening FRC, calibrates them against laboratory tests, and turns them into practical design tools such as interaction curves for members under combined axial load and bending.

Research focus

  • Post-cracking tensile behaviour of steel-fibre and self-compacting concretes
  • Strain-softening vs strain-hardening material models
  • Design curves for FRC members under axial load and bending moment
  • Cost-effective steel-fibre-reinforced self-compacting concrete for structural use
  • Implementation of material models in nonlinear finite element codes
Theme 02

FRP strengthening & cyclic behaviour of RC structures

Much of India's existing building and bridge stock needs strengthening for higher loads or for seismic demands. Carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) sheets and strips offer a lightweight way to confine columns and increase flexural capacity without large changes in member size.

Dr Varma's doctoral work at the University of Minho combined large-scale tests on RC columns with numerical models of CFRP-confined concrete under monotonic and cyclic compression, and a 3D Timoshenko fibre model for frames implemented in the finite element software FEMIX. More recent work reviews the performance of near-surface-mounted (NSM) CFRP laminates bonded into slits.

Research focus

  • Constitutive models for CFRP-confined concrete under cyclic loading
  • Near-surface-mounted CFRP strips and laminates for flexural strengthening of columns
  • Fibre (fibrous) models for 3D RC frames under cyclic actions
  • Large-scale experimental testing of strengthened RC columns
  • Bond and debonding of NSM-CFRP laminates in slits
Theme 03

Architected materials & lightweight metastructures

Architected materials derive their properties from geometry as much as from the base material. By designing the shape of cells in a lattice — honeycombs, curved and anti-curvature walls, Voronoi architectures — it is possible to obtain combinations of stiffness, strength and energy absorption that solid materials cannot reach.

The group couples physics-based mechanics with machine learning to understand how lattices fail and to design them for strength, stiffness and energy absorption. This work has supported sponsored projects with DST on metamaterial panel walls for acoustic and seismic resistance, and with ARDB–DRDO on graded lattice-infilled tubes for energy absorption.

Research focus

  • Failure-pattern-driven strength enhancement of honeycomb lattices using machine learning
  • Anti-curvature lattices for optimally enhanced specific strength, stiffness and energy absorption
  • Effect of structural imperfections on lattice behaviour
  • Machine-learning-guided reinforced Voronoi porous architectures
  • Smart soft-elastomer structures with electro-magnetic actuation

Theme 04

Wave propagation & dynamics of micropolar composite panels

Classical continuum theories ignore the internal length scales of composite and periodic materials. Micropolar (Cosserat) theory adds independent micro-rotations, which lets models capture size effects and extra wave modes that matter at higher frequencies.

This theme develops spectral element and transfer-matrix formulations for homogeneous, layered and periodic micropolar-Cosserat panels. The models reveal band gaps — frequency ranges in which waves cannot travel — and phenomena such as mode veering, locking and coupling, with applications in vibration isolation and acoustic design.

Research focus

  • Spectral element formulations for micropolar-Cosserat panels
  • Band gaps and flexural wave propagation in periodic composite panels
  • Damped, transversely isotropic and layered composite panels
  • Mode veering, locking and coupling; criteria for mode-shape tracking
  • Validation against commercial finite element models

Theme 05

Wind engineering for hilly terrain

Wind accelerates as it flows over hills and escarpments, so buildings and structures on exposed slopes can see much higher loads than design codes assume for flat ground. This is especially relevant in Jammu & Kashmir and other Himalayan regions, where construction on hill slopes is common.

Using a boundary-layer wind tunnel, the group measures wind speed and turbulence profiles over hills and escarpments of different slopes, and the forces on building models placed near and on top of them. The results are used to assess the topography factor in the Indian Standard and to recommend safer building locations in hilly terrain.

Research focus

  • Wind flow over gentle and steep escarpments and two-dimensional hills
  • Local topography factors for design wind speed and comparison with Indian Standards
  • Along-wind forces, base shear and pressures on buildings in hilly terrain
  • Effect of escarpment steepness on wind pressure distribution
  • Wind effects on structures in hilly terrain (ongoing doctoral research)

Theme 06

Precast prestressed concrete pavement panels

Roads in high-altitude and remote areas have short construction seasons and harsh climates. Precast prestressed concrete pavement panels are cast and cured under controlled conditions, then placed quickly on site — offering faster construction, better quality and longer service life than conventional cast-in-place pavements.

This theme, supported by the Border Roads Organisation, studies the behaviour of precast prestressed panels and their joints under repeated traffic loading using a custom-built cyclic loading set-up, with the aim of developing guidelines for their design and use on strategic roads.

Research focus

  • Design of precast prestressed pavement panels for hill roads
  • Behaviour of panels and joints under cyclic (repeated) loading
  • Custom experimental set-up for cyclic load testing
  • Guidelines and standards for design, construction and maintenance
  • Rapid construction for short working seasons in mountainous regions
